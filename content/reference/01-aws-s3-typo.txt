On February 28, 2017, one typo took a big piece of the internet offline for about 4 hours.

An AWS engineer was debugging S3's billing system in us-east-1. It's routine work. The command should've removed a few servers. One input was wrong, so it removed far more, including servers that two core S3 subsystems couldn't run without, and S3 in that region stopped answering.

Sites like Slack, Trello and Quora broke. Even the AWS Service Health Dashboard couldn't update, because it lived on S3 too.

Why your app would've failed:
→ S3's index subsystem knows where each of your objects lives.
→ Without it, your GETs fail.
→ It wasn't a quick restart. The index had to check a huge pile of metadata before anyone could trust it again, and at that size it takes hours.
→ AWS said those subsystems hadn't been fully restarted in many years.

It's like a library losing its catalogue. Your book's still on the shelf, exactly where it was, and you've got absolutely no way to find it until someone rebuilds the whole index.

The fix was simple. AWS made the tool remove capacity slowly and refuse to go below a safe minimum.

Takeaway: if your tool can delete capacity, give it a speed limit and a floor.

Which command in your system is one typo away from doing this to you?
