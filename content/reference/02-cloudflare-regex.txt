On July 2, 2019, one regular expression pushed Cloudflare's CPUs to 100% across the world.

You'd think a global outage needs a giant bug. Not this one. A single new firewall rule reached every Cloudflare server within seconds, and for about 27 minutes a huge share of the sites behind Cloudflare returned 502 errors to people like you.

What went wrong:
→ The rule used a regex that backtracks.
→ Backtracking means the engine keeps retrying different ways to match, and on some inputs that's millions of tries for one short string.
→ Every request hit the rule. Every CPU stalled.
→ The change didn't get the slow rollout your code would get, so nothing caught it first.

Picture a cashier who recounts every coin in the drawer before each sale. You won't notice. The 10,000 people in line behind you will.

The fix: Cloudflare switched to a regex engine that can't backtrack like that. It also stopped pushing rule changes everywhere at once.

Takeaway: if it runs on every request, your config deserves the same staged rollout as your code.

Do you roll out config changes as carefully as deploys?
